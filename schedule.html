Week 1 · Wednesday 2 September 2026
playground.html

Tool: Codespace, edit, commit, push

Example: a simple web page as a laboratory notebook. HTML is structure, CSS is appearance, JavaScript is behaviour.

18:00 Introduction
The three layers of a page. Then about ten minutes on repository, commit, and push.
18:25 Demonstration
Instructor changes one layer in a Codespace and commits. github.dev is the fallback.
18:40 Exercise
Edit the heading, a colour, and the button so it reports a time in milliseconds.
19:25 Results
In lab-notes/week01.md, state which layer produced your change.
20:05 Modification
Arts: presentation of the card. Science: record two times and show the difference. Disclose any AI use.
Preparation
Week 0 complete. Laptop that can sign in to GitHub.

Report: screenshot, named layer, disclosure. Push by Tuesday 17:00.

Week 2 · Wednesday 9 September 2026
rt.html

Tool: collect a timed response and export a CSV

Example: simple reaction time (press when a mark appears) and optional choice reaction time (two colours). Anticipations under 150 ms and lapses over 2500 ms are excluded from the mean.

18:00 Introduction
What a reaction time is. Write a predicted mean before the first trial.
18:25 Demonstration
Instructor runs a short block and exports the CSV. Do not interpret a 6-trial mean as a finding.
18:40 Exercise
Simple RT block (about 20 usable trials). Students are the participants.
19:25 Results
Report n, mean, and standard deviation. Save the CSV in data/.
20:05 Modification
Arts: change the appearance of the mark. Science: run the two-colour (choice) block and compare the two means.
Reading
A short note on Donders (1868) or a one-page instructor primer. Skim.

Report: prediction, mean RT, n, one limitation (practice, anticipation, fatigue). Push the report and the CSV.

Week 3 · Wednesday 16 September 2026
page to be added

Tool: a form, a score, and a data table

Example: a short personality or mood inventory (for example a 10-item Big Five excerpt). Items, a Likert scale, a total or subscale score, CSV.

18:00 Introduction
Self-report as data, not as a diagnosis. Write a predicted score range.
18:25 Demonstration
Complete the scale once as a class walk-through. Export.
18:40 Exercise
Students complete the inventory and save their table.
19:25 Results
Report the score and what it is (and is not) a measure of.
20:05 Modification
Arts: wording and layout of the items. Science: add one reverse-scored item or a second subscale.
Reading
A short Big Five or scale-construction primer from the instructor.

Report: score, item count, one limitation of self-report. CSV in data/.

Week 4 · Wednesday 23 September 2026
page to be added

18:00 Introduction
A number without a spread is incomplete.
18:25 Demonstration

18:40 Exercise
Apply the same calculations to your own file.
19:25 Results
Report mean, SD, and n. Note any values you excluded and why.
20:05 Modification
Arts: the figure. Science: a second descriptive (median or a split by first vs second half).
Reading
