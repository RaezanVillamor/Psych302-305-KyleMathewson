Week 2 · Wednesday 9 September 2026
search.html

Visual search and reaction time

RT = a + bN

Collect reaction times while searching for a red T. Feature search (colour alone) is expected to stay near 0 ms per extra item. Conjunction search (colour and shape) is expected to take longer as set size increases.

18:00 Introduction
Two predicted lines on the board before anyone responds.
18:25 Demonstration
Instructor illustration data only (about 1 vs 25 ms/item), then a 6-trial practice. Do not interpret practice slopes.
18:40 Exercise
Full 36-trial block. Students are the participants.
19:25 Results
Report both slopes. One sentence: did your data match the prediction?
20:05 Modification
Arts: change colour and background; rerun a short block. Science: propose a target-absent key or a third set size.
Reading
Treisman and Gelade (1980), first four pages and Figure 1. Wolfe (2020) abstract is sufficient if the paper is long.

Report: both slopes, your 18:10 prediction, one reason a slope can mislead (practice, lapses, grouping). Save the CSV in data/ and push both files.

Week 3 · Wednesday 16 September 2026
sdt.html

Signal detection

d′ = z(Hit) − z(False alarm)

Percent correct confuses sensitivity with the decision to say “yes.” Tonight separates the two.

18:00 Introduction
Two overlapping distributions. Criterion is a policy, not an ability.
18:25 Demonstration
Hold d′ fixed and move criterion. Percent correct changes; sensitivity does not.
18:40 Exercise
20 trials. J = yes, F = no. Compute your d′ and c.
19:25 Results
Compare the demonstration to your own criterion. Liberal or conservative?
20:05 Modification
Arts: redesign the noise field. Science: add a second contrast and predict which d′ changes.
Reading
Stanislaw and Todorov (1999), the worked example. Skip the software appendix.

Report: your d′ and c, and what would happen to “accuracy” if you simply said yes more often.

Week 4 · Wednesday 23 September 2026
page to be added: staircase.html

Thresholds

next contrast = last contrast ± step · (correct ? −1 : +1)

A 1-up/1-down staircase on the same red T. The rule tracks the edge of detection.

18:00 Introduction
Threshold as a moving estimate. Difference between 1-up/1-down and a 70.7% rule.
18:25 Demonstration
Watch one staircase and identify a reversal.
18:40 Exercise
40 trials. Plot contrast by trial. Mean of the last six reversals.
19:25 Results
Report the threshold. Did the walk settle or wander?
20:05 Modification
Arts: change the background so the threshold should move. Science: switch to 2-down/1-up and predict the new equilibrium.
Reading
Kingdom and Prins, short staircase chapter, or Levitt (1971), first three pages.

Report: threshold, rule, and whether a different background would have changed the number. Due Tuesday 29 September (no class on the 30th).

Week 5 · Wednesday 7 October 2026
page to be added: blink.html

Rapid serial presentation

P(T2 correct | T1 correct) = f(lag)

Two targets in a stream. Accuracy as a function of lag (a short attentional blink).

18:00 Introduction
Why a second target is often missed at 200–500 ms. Draw the lag function.
18:25 Demonstration
One slow stream, one full-speed stream.
18:40 Exercise
Lags 1, 3, and 8. Enough trials for a sketch, not a paper.
19:25 Results
Three points. Is there a dip, lag-1 sparing, or mainly fatigue?
20:05 Modification
Arts: restyle the stream. Science: add T1-absent catch trials and state their purpose.
Reading
Raymond, Shapiro, and Arnell (1992), abstract and Figure 1.

Report: three lag points and whether you trust the dip. Due Tuesday 13 October (Thanksgiving is Monday).
