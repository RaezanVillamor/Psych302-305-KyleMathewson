Introduction to Computational Methods in Psychology

Laboratory handbook

This course teaches computing tools used in psychological research: a GitHub repository, a browser editor, version control, a short data file, and a written report. The examples are ordinary psychology: reaction time, a personality inventory, descriptive statistics, and research documentation.

PSYCH 302 and PSYCH 305 meet together. Canvas is the official course. This site is the handbook and the working laboratory pages.

Tools

What you learn to do

Open a Codespace, edit a page, commit and push, collect a CSV, compute a mean, cite a paper, and write Methods and Results in the repository. Copilot may be used if it is free; every report discloses that use.

Examples

Classic psychology

Simple and choice reaction time, a short inventory, a two-condition comparison, and a methods-and-results page. The course is not a survey of mathematical psychology.

Arts and Science

Modification hour

After the common exercise, Arts students may improve presentation and wording. Science students may add a table or a comparison. Both are graded on a clear prediction and a visible result.

Assessment

Weights

50% weekly laboratory reports (weeks 1–9; drop the lowest).
15% individual midterm check-in (28 October): question, taught tool, one prediction, and what already exists in the repository.
35% individual final project (2 December): a small study, a methods-and-results page, or a browser game that studies a question. Self as participant by default. The page is the submission.
There is no graded group project and no graded peer review. Reports include a prediction, a result, a limitation, and an AI-use disclosure.
